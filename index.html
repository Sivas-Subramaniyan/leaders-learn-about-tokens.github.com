Chapter 1 — The Meter

How the meter actually clicks

You'd expect the taxi meter to tick per word. It doesn't. It ticks per sub-word fragment — a unit called a token, decided by the model's internal vocabulary. Short common words cost one click. Long or rare words split into multiple clicks.

"The meter doesn't care about your grammar. It counts syllable-sized chunks of text — whether you're writing a polished brief or a typo-riddled note."
Token Principle #1

This matters because verbose, wordy prompts rack up the meter faster — not just in word count, but because complex vocabulary splits into more fragments. Technical jargon, long words, and multiple languages all cost more per character.

Chapter 2 — Directions vs Miles

The two sides of every fare

Every cab ride has two costs: the briefing before you set off, and the actual miles driven. AI works the same way. What you send to the model costs one rate. What it generates back costs another — and it's significantly higher.

🗺 Model Tiering
Route simple tasks to the auto-rickshaw and escalate complex ones to the sedan. This single architectural decision cuts enterprise AI spend by 60–80% with no quality loss. The routing logic itself costs almost nothing.
🔁 Prompt Caching
Like a driver who already knows your office address — he doesn't need directions again. Cached prompts (the same system context sent repeatedly) cost 90% less on repeat calls. One-time setup, permanent savings.
📋 The Briefing Card
Every driver gets a printed briefing card (system prompt) before starting. A 2,000-token card sent 50,000 times/month = 100M tokens in overhead alone. Write it once, write it tight.

Chapter 6 — Smarter Directions

Same destination. Wildly different fares.

How you phrase your directions to the driver changes the meter reading dramatically — even for the same errand. Three people can ask for the same analysis and generate three very different token bills.

❌ Verbose — 10-minute verbal briefing
Hello, I hope this message finds you well. I wanted to reach out today because I have a task that I need your assistance with. Our company, Deloitte, is a professional services firm and we work with many clients across various industries. I would like you to act as a senior consultant and help me analyze the following situation. The client is a large financial services company and they are considering whether they should adopt artificial intelligence technology in their operations. Could you please provide a comprehensive analysis covering all relevant aspects including strategic considerations, risks, opportunities, implementation challenges, and any other factors you think might be important? Please be thorough and detailed in your response as this is very important. The client has mentioned that they are particularly interested in understanding the cost implications, the talent requirements, and the timeline for implementation.

What went wrong in the cab
  • Pleasantries ("I hope this finds you well") cost tokens. The AI doesn't care. The meter does.
  • "Could you please" and "I would like you to" are verbal filler — identical output without them.
  • Repeating "please be thorough" doesn't improve quality. It costs 6 tokens and changes nothing.
  • Identity context ("Deloitte is a professional services firm") belongs in the driver briefing card (system prompt) — sent once, not every ride.
  • The vague "any other factors" invitation costs tokens and produces bloated output.

Chapter 7 — The Driver's Memory

When the cab driver forgets where you started

Every AI model has a context window — the total tokens it can hold in working memory at once. Think of it as the driver's mental dashboard. Overfill it, and they start forgetting earlier parts of the journey. Manage it strategically.

🚕 The Driver's Mental Dashboard

Imagine your cab driver's working memory is a 128,000-click dashboard. As the ride progresses, the dashboard fills up: your initial directions, the map you shared, everything said during the journey, space reserved for what they'll say next. When it fills up, they start forgetting what you said at the beginning. At enterprise scale, this means your AI starts giving incomplete answers.
