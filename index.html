Module 01 — Foundations

What exactly is a token?

Not a word. Not a character. A token is a sub-word unit — the atomic piece of text that language models process. Understanding this unlocks everything about AI costs, capabilities, and strategy.

01
📝

Tokens ≠ Words

Common short words are 1 token. Long or rare words split into multiple tokens. The word "Deloitte" is likely 2 tokens; "AI" is 1.

Deloi tte = 2 tokens
AI = 1 token
02
💰

Tokens = Dollars

Every token you send (input) and receive (output) is billed. A 3-page consulting brief is ~2,000 tokens. At scale, this adds up — or can be optimized dramatically.

1M tokens ≈ $3.00 Claude Sonnet
03
🔄

Input vs Output

What you send to the model (prompt, context, documents) are input tokens. What it generates back are output tokens — and they cost 4× more to produce.

INPUT → $3/1M tokens
OUTPUT → $15/1M tokens
The Partner Insight

The "Metered Taxi" Mental Model

Think of tokens like a taxi meter that runs on both ends of the trip. You pay to tell the driver where to go (input tokens) AND you pay for every mile of the route (output tokens). A verbose, poorly structured prompt is like giving the driver a 10-minute verbal briefing before starting. Expert clients write clear, precise instructions — and get charged accordingly.

🎯

Right-size the model

Routing simple tasks (data extraction, formatting) to fast/cheap models and complex reasoning to power models can cut costs by 60–80% with no quality loss. This is "model tiering" — a core AI strategy.

📦

Caching = free tokens

Most providers offer prompt caching: if you send the same system prompt or context repeatedly (e.g., a 10K-token policy document), you pay 90% less for those cached tokens on repeat calls.

🔁

Agents multiply costs

An AI agent that reasons in loops (plan → act → observe → repeat) processes tokens at every step. A 10-step agent loop multiplies your token bill by 10×. Agentic architectures require cost-aware design.

📄

The system prompt tax

Every API call includes a system prompt (persona, rules, context). A 2,000-token system prompt sent 50,000 times/month = 100M tokens just in overhead. Optimize it once, save millions of tokens.

Module 05 — Strategic Optimization

The Art of Token Efficiency

The same task, written three ways, produces wildly different token counts — and costs. This is where AI strategy becomes business strategy.

❌ Verbose — Typical First Attempt
Hello, I hope this message finds you well. I wanted to reach out today because I have a task that I need your assistance with. Our company, Deloitte, is a professional services firm and we work with many clients across various industries. I would like you to act as a senior consultant and help me analyze the following situation. The client is a large financial services company and they are considering whether they should adopt artificial intelligence technology in their operations. Could you please provide a comprehensive analysis covering all relevant aspects including strategic considerations, risks, opportunities, implementation challenges, and any other factors you think might be important? Please be thorough and detailed in your response as this is very important. The client has mentioned that they are particularly interested in understanding the cost implications, the talent requirements, and the timeline for implementation. Please make sure to cover all of these points in your analysis.

What's wrong here?
  • Pleasantries ("I hope this message finds you well") cost tokens and add no value to the AI
  • "Could you please" and "I would like you to" are filler. The model doesn't need social cues.
  • Repeating "please make sure" and "please be thorough" don't improve output quality
  • The vague instruction "any other factors" invites unnecessarily verbose output
  • The identity context ("Deloitte is a professional services firm") is better in the system prompt once, not every call
